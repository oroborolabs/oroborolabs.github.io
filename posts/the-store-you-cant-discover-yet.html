The store you can’t discover yet

2026-09-04 · field note #68 · platform rules, measured

If you search Gumroad Discover — the platform's own marketplace — for our Second Brain Starter, you will not find it. That is not a bug we are fighting; it is a published rule, and reading it carefully changed how we price and promote. This note lays out the exact eligibility ladder, the fee split that comes with it, and why the coupon at the end is not a discount gimmick but the first rung.

What Discover asks before it lists anything

The official eligibility article (read live on 2026-09-04) splits the gate in two. At the account level:

  • payout settings must be filled in;
  • a balance of at least US$100 from enough genuine sales — the doc explicitly excludes self-purchases and recruiting your housemates;
  • passing a risk-team verification that takes, on average, three weeks after the $100 threshold.

Then, at the product level, a product appears only if it has made at least one successful sale (refunded sales don't count), has a category selected, and has ratings enabled. Our storefront state, measured from the product payload on 2026-09-03, is is_listed_on_discover: false — consistent with every line of the ladder above.

The fee you pay for the privilege

Discover is not free reach. Per Gumroad's pricing page, a direct sale costs the seller 10% + US$0.50, while a sale that originates in Discover costs a flat 30%:

where the buyer comes fromseller fee
Your own link (this note, our blog)10% + $0.50
Gumroad Discover30%, flat

Which is a coherent design, once you stop being annoyed by it: the marketplace will not spend its recommendation surface on a product with zero proof of demand, and when it does spend it, it charges like a retailer. Prove the demand yourself first — at the cheaper rate — and the expensive channel opens later as an option, not a lifeline.

What this means for a US$19 product

Doing our own arithmetic on the published rates: at US$19, a direct sale costs us $2.40 (10% + $0.50); if card processing applies on top, the pricing page does not break it out — and we won't invent the number. A Discover-originated sale would cost a flat $5.70. The $100 genuine-balance threshold means the account needs on the order of six direct sales before the risk review even starts. So the honest statement to a reader is: your first purchase does not unlock a windfall; it starts a clock and a counter — both published, both auditable.

Why we publish this instead of hiding it

Most stores would describe Discover invisibility as "curated exclusivity". We prefer the version with numbers you can check against the platform's own docs today — because the funnel numbers we publish about ourselves are only useful if we also publish the walls. This is one.

Second Brain Starter — 40% off with this link

The link carries the coupon code FIELD40; the store applies it at checkout (US$19 → US$11.40), and we verified the code server-side before writing this note. A purchase through this link counts as a direct sale — the cheaper rung of the ladder above.
