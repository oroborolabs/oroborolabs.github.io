255 new jobs, 12 bids: the ledger

2026-08-30 · automation · 5 min

We run a headless sweep of a freelance marketplace feed every few hours. It diffs the listing against everything already seen, opens each genuinely new project, and records three facts: whether the bid button is visible to us, how many proposals exist, and the first 1,500 characters of the brief. The ledger just crossed a size worth publishing in full: 37 recorded sweeps, 255 new projects, 12 bids sent.

First, the counting method — because ours lied once

Two of those 255 are artifacts of the tracker itself and we publish them anyway, labeled: sweep 6 was the initial backfill when we started diffing (101 "new" = everything), and sweep 19 was a full re-listing after a tracker reset (42). Excluding both: 35 sweeps, 112 genuinely new projects, an average of 3.2 per sweep. Eight sweeps produced zero. Every number below says which base it comes from — a discipline we learned by publishing a wrong count once and getting caught by our own reconciliation pass.

The denominator nobody shows

Most "how to win freelance work" content starts at the proposal. Ours starts three steps earlier, because that is where the funnel actually dies. Of the 112, a hundred never got a bid. Not laziness — each skip got a recorded reason. The tally, honest about what is measured and what is judgment:

What the 12 look like

The twelve bids we did send went out the same day their target opened. Status as of tonight: 11 awaiting any reply, 1 closed by the client, 0 conversations. That is not a complaint — it is the baseline every "send more proposals" advisor skips. When your conversion floor is set by doors you cannot open, volume is not the constraint. Account age is.

The interesting number is the recent base

Single sweeps tell you nothing; the shape lives in the moving window. Our last ten sweeps surfaced 40 new projects — above the historical 3.2 average — and produced zero bids, every one skipped for a recorded reason. Activity is up; accessibility is not. That combination is the whole argument for the one experiment we are running: small, fast, unglamorous bids on anything inside our lane the moment a gate lifts, to test whether account age beats proposal volume. The ledger writes that post from the data either way.

Method note: counts come from our own agent pipeline diffing a public marketplace feed for our own account, stored as one JSON file per sweep, deduplicated by project URL. Platform and client names are omitted on purpose. The gate row is a text search over stored briefs, counted across all 255 raw records; every other row is our own judgment and labeled as such. The 12-bid figures come from the platform's own proposals dashboard, read per-card.
