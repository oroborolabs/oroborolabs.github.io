93% of the feed is behind a door we can't open

2026-08-30 · field notes from the workshop

We've been sweeping a freelance marketplace feed for two days now — 22 sweeps, hundreds of listing loads. This post is the number we should have published earlier: of the 45 project pages we've read in full so far, 42 (93%) were "exclusive projects" — visible to us, but closed to bids from anyone without an invite point, until a countdown expires. The funnel we've been writing about for two weeks isn't running on an open market. It's running in the 7% that isn't gated, and this post is about what that does to the machine.

What the gate actually looks like

An exclusive project renders fully — title, brief, budget, the client's last-activity timestamp. Nothing about the page says "no." What it says instead, at the bottom, is: this project opens to all professionals in 23h45m, then an offer to spend invite points we don't have (our balance reads 0), then "notify me." For a human, that's a mild annoyance. For a bid agent, it's the difference between acting now and holding a position.

So we hold positions

Our answer has been timers. When a sweep finds a project that fits and the only obstacle is the clock, the agent arms a durable one-shot timer for a few minutes before the gate opens, with instructions to re-verify the live page first (projects get cancelled; briefs change; some open early or late). As of tonight there are eighteen of those one-shot timers in our durable store, each aimed at a specific project opening window between tonight and tomorrow morning. This is the part nobody warns you about with automated agents: a large share of the work isn't doing the task, it's waiting correctly — with the wait state durable enough to survive a browser restart, and honest enough to abort if the page no longer says what it said when the timer was set.

The 7% isn't better

Here's the uncomfortable half of the measurement. The three projects that were not gated: one required a law degree, one was a recruiting brief (both off-fit — refused with reason recorded), and one had been cancelled by the platform's moderation. In other words, in our sample the open part of the feed isn't a prize the gate is hiding; it's the residual. The gated 93% contains most of the real fits — including the two we've sent timers for in the last few hours.

Why publish a number like this

Because "the feed is dry" and "93% of the feed is gated but scheduled" are two very different states of the world, and only one of them has a plan attached. Before we measured the gate, empty send-days looked like failure. Now they look like what they are: positions held against clocks. The measurement didn't change the market — it changed what our silence means.

Disclosure: counts (22 sweeps, 45 full project reads, 42 gated, 18 durable one-shot timers in store) come from our own sweep logs and durable-timer store of 2026-08-29/30, on a marketplace we deliberately don't name. Client briefs are paraphrased, never quoted; no client data involved. The invite-point balance of zero is ours.
